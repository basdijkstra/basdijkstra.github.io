Get [more valuable feedback](/the-valuable-in-valuable-feedback-fast/), faster, from your existing test automation by identifying and addressing the problems that make your tests slow, brittle, expensive or ineffective.

The goal of test automation is to provide fast, reliable, meaningful feedback that helps development teams build and release software with confidence. But as applications, test suites and teams grow, test automation can become increasingly difficult to maintain and increasingly slow at providing useful feedback.

Many organisations respond by throwing more tests, more tools or more infrastructure at the problem. However, this can often make things worse rather than better.

The problem is often not that you don't have enough automated tests. It is that your existing tests aren't providing the right feedback at the right time.

I can help you change that.

## Where the test automation optimization program helps

This engagement is particularly useful when:

* Your automated tests take too long to provide useful feedback
* Your end-to-end test suite has become large, slow or difficult to maintain
* You are struggling with a significant number of flaky tests
* Your teams spend too much time investigating test failures that don't indicate real problems
* You have duplication between unit, integration, and end-to-end tests
* You aren't sure whether your current distribution of tests is appropriate for your architecture
* Your test automation suite has grown organically and you are wondering whether it is still fit for purpose
* You want to improve your test automation efforts, but don't know which problems to tackle first

## Is adding more tests really the answer?

Adding more automated tests doesn't automatically give you more confidence.

Before recommending new tools, technologies or testing techniques, we'll look at your product architecture, your existing test automation suite, your development process and the feedback your teams actually receive from their tests.

We'll find an answer to questions such as:

* How quickly do developers get useful feedback after making a change?
* Which tests provide the most valuable feedback?
* Where are you relying too heavily on end-to-end tests?
* Which tests are slow, flaky or expensive to maintain?
* Where are different types of tests duplicating each other's work?
* Where are important defects or integration problems not being detected early enough?
* Are your test suites structured in a way that fits your architecture and development process?
* Where can feedback be moved closer to the developer?

The goal is to help your teams get valuable feedback, fast, not to maximize the number of automated tests.
